# R7 — Mixed goods and installation journey

**2026-10-14 · Illustrates accepted H1–H8/0130–0132; no adapter or correction workflow delivered.**
The preserved example applies only with authoritatively checked downstream impacts; delivered quantity
is not the only possible lower bound. [Specified handoff](phase-4-r7-implementation-slices.md).

## 1. Offer, consent and readiness

A distributor sells100 stocked controllers at10.0000 each, plus one complete installation package
at200.0000 covering those100 controllers. Both scopes and their dependency are explicit. The agreed
package is FULL_ONLY, not freely divisible by controller count. Goods are a direct Item, installation
is Assembly-backed with pinned technical/formulation/material versions and source controls.

Issued quotation Q1: goods net1000.0000, installation net200.0000, total net1200.0000,
output tax120.0000, gross1320.0000. Customer PO plus verified representative confirmation commits to
**all100 goods and one installation**, with deliveries planned40 then60. R6 records one accepted
case/consent component with two accepted lines. No new acceptance at dispatch or Job creation.
Original issued quantities, consent, component allocations, FX and tax estimate remain immutable.

If the customer had committed only40, normal recording cannot claim100 or bulk entitlement. It must
follow explicit R6 partial entitlement or new reoffer/fresh consent. That is a different transaction.

Execution-only site access and team details are initially missing. Accepted business remains valid;
readiness is blocked for installation. Any missing destination fact that determines contractual site,
price or tax would instead have been a commercial prerequisite before authoritative acceptance.

Installation uses delivered controllers already accounted for by the goods flow. It must link their

allocation just to show that link. If an implementation uses Order→Job for the same service obligation,
that is a stage edge referencing AI1, not two parallel assignments of1. Dependency on goods quantity
is a link, not permission to add100 controllers plus1 package into a meaningless101 total.

show attributed commercial amounts, always marked as source-linked; summing them with accepted value

## 3. Readiness and first delivery

Goods require an eligible fulfilment warehouse/location, real destination, supported package/UoM/
traceability path and appropriate release authority. Current usable stock may be checked; it is not
reserved until a separately supported Inventory reservation step succeeds. SO1 release does not post
stock. Pick/move/dispatch use the Inventory-owned authorised boundary and protected scope.

Installation requires site, team/instructions, relevant technical baseline, dependency coverage and
applicable controls. A preparer may resolve instructions; required reviewer approves exact reviewed
facts when policy requires; releaser checks current readiness. Acceptance recorder, operational
preparer/approver/releaser, Inventory poster and sensitive commercial corrector are separate duties.
No universal CustomerAccount or maker/checker is introduced; R9 chooses names, scope and grants.

First batch planned40, actually customer-delivered30. Record actual30 with protected source evidence;
stock dispatched and customer delivered could differ in a real case and must have separate facts.
For this example both are30. The first batch has10 backordered; second batch remains60. Total
remaining goods obligation70, not an additional acceptance70 and not a new priced offer70.
Installation package remains1 outstanding, not30% completed merely because goods arrived.

Moving an internal slot within the agreed delivery window is operational scheduling. Moving to a
new contractual destination, missing an agreed deadline or changing a site-dependent technical/tax
scope needs customer agreement and Sales correction where applicable. A carrier's reschedule is
not itself consent. Holds or actual delay cannot be relabelled as customer rejection/cancellation.

## 6. Duplicate handover and later cancellation

Another employee retries HP1 with a fresh request key after A1. Current authority/read eligibility
still applies. Stable HP1/AG1/AI1 stage identity resolves SO1/J1, with original result IDs and current
safe status; stale original100 payload cannot silently recreate demand. Changed intent conflicts.

A person invents HP2 to allocate the same full goods100: identity review/capacity checks refuse.
A genuine additional operational split must identify disjoint remaining scope under the current80
cap. Genuine additional customer purchase needs a separate verified R6 decision, not a new Job ID.

If a legacy duplicate empty Job exists, cancelling it records an operational correction and releases
only its verified unconsumed assignment. It does not cancel the accepted package or release any
commercial/exclusive claim. If J1 was the valid sole target, cancelling/replacing it still needs
proper reassignment/dependency controls and leaves installation commercially outstanding.

Full cancellation, return of delivered30 or switching accepted family is outside the bounded
reduction. Sales must authorise commercial change/release; Ops handles targets; Inventory handles
stock/reservations/returns; Finance handles invoices/credits/payments/tax/COGS. Missing adapter means
pending/refused execution, not automatic release. Reduced or zero operational workload is not proof
that commercial liabilities and exclusive bindings are extinguished.

Unassigned versus unscheduled are different states. In a fully assigned path, active-unfulfilled
allocation plus completed allocated quantity equals assigned root quantity; target stage totals are
not added again. Remaining obligation = effective agreed quantity minus its authoritative completed
quantity for this simple no-return case. Returns, credits, substitutions and service progress require
their own approved semantics; do not import this subtraction as a universal accounting formula.
Commercial value comes once from acceptance plus valid amendments. Assignment/schedule/actual ledgers
answer different questions; none independently increases accepted sales or publishes a trusted forecast.
