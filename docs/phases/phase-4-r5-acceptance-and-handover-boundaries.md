## Commitment provenance and concurrency requirements for later R6/R7

Acceptance needs original family/revision/logical-scope/option and allocation IDs, requested and
cumulative accepted quantities, original versus agreed terms, a stable customer-decision/intent
reference with customer evidence and occurrence/
recording time, actor/reason/authority, conditional-tier outcome, component/FX/policy links and
remaining claim generation. Retried key/payload returns original outcome; a different key cannot
re-accept the same customer decision/allocation or exhausted quantity, or make an incompatible
option/exclusive-family claim. R6 must settle business decision identity so two retry keys cannot
consume40 twice while the total remains below100. Distinct genuine purchases are not duplicates
merely because their amounts/quantities match; they require distinct supported decision evidence.

Handover needs authoritative accepted allocation plus target intent, quantities and original/agreed
commercial/source/estimate/cost certainty/contact/site evidence. Separate receipt from acceptance.
Enforce business uniqueness/consumed-scope bounds as well as idempotency so a fresh retry key cannot
create another target for the same handover intent. R7 must decide one-vs-many targets, disjoint splits,
Order→Job downstream links and safe corrections; do not mistake linked fulfilment stages for twice
accepted business. Multiple jobs are not permitted by this research. No issue-time reservation,
Finance posting or universal customer-account gate is implied.

Acceptance, replacement, withdrawal, exclusive-group membership and commitment adjustments must
share stable scope/claim coordination, including ungrouped families. Concurrent absence of a row
is not a lock protocol. One-actionable overlapping revision scope is within a family; accepted0100 competing families
remain available alternatives governed by their exclusive acceptance claim. R5 owns
issue/history/availability; R6 accepted claim/allocation; R7 target
baseline. Required company/record guards and database conservation protect all three; R9 determines
exact duties. A retry must not silently choose a newer offer or release an existing exclusive claim.
